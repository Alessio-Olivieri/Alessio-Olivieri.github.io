What does encrypted traffic actually reveal?

Reached 92.53% mean accuracy on a fixed flow-disjoint split, then used payload randomization and capture-provenance analysis to investigate what the classifier had actually learned.

A high score is the start of the investigation

The research question was whether packet payloads help classify encrypted traffic because of ciphertext byte values, or because the payload also contains lengths, framing, cleartext and other structure.

I adapted the TFE-GNN architecture to PyTorch Geometric and evaluated six ISCXVPN2016 traffic classes. The model uses separate header and payload graphs, GraphSAGE encoders, cross-gated fusion and a bidirectional LSTM. The architecture originates with Zhang et al., WWW 2023.

My contribution

I connected the adapted model to packet extraction and graph construction, introduced controlled payload and TLS randomization experiments, and traced predictions back to capture provenance. Saved predictions, split definitions, result verification and regression tests make the analysis inspectable without retraining.

What the controls revealed

Replacing complete payloads reduced average accuracy and increased variability. Randomizing only conservatively identified TLS 1.2 AES-GCM ciphertext did not reduce the measured score. That supports a narrower conclusion than “the model understands encryption”: useful payload information is not necessarily in the ciphertext values.

Coverage matters. The TLS intervention changed 17.97% of retained payload bytes and touched 85 of 250 test samples. Unsupported bytes and protocol structure remained. This is an observed contrast, not proof that ciphertext never matters.

The evaluation limit I found

The 1,172/252/250 train/validation/test split is flow-disjoint, but its partitions share capture sources. The capture audit found too few independent acquisition groups to support a defensible six-class capture-independent evaluation. Three training seeds measure training variability; they do not create independent test data.

The outcome is both a working classifier and a documented boundary on its claims. No capture-independent generalization score is reported.
