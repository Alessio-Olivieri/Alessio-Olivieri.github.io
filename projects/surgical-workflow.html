Recognize the workflow, not just individual frames

A model can label most frames correctly and still fragment a surgical procedure into implausible short phases. Our benchmark compared frame-level classification with sequence and segment quality on Cholec80, using whole videos as the unit of separation.

We evaluated seven model variants against three ResNet50 feature sources: recurrent models, a windowed Transformer, multi-task MS-TCN and SF-TMN with an ASFormer backbone.

My contribution within the team

I contributed to the benchmark and evaluation protocol, analyzed the comparison of temporal architectures and feature sources, and presented the quantitative results, rankings and trade-offs. Feature extraction and model development were shared work; the reported performance belongs to the four-person project.

Team: Christian Corvino, Giorgia Chiaro, Marta Cudia and Alessio Olivieri. Sapienza University of Rome, Medical Robotics, July 2026.

A held-out protocol

  • 80 procedures, 184,578 sampled frames: one frame per second, with seven surgical phase labels.
  • 32 training / 8 validation / 40 test videos: entire videos remain separate between partitions.
  • Validation-led selection: checkpoints, losses and postprocessing are selected using validation, followed by the final held-out test.

The result that changed the ranking

MS-TCN with 11 layers and the teammates’ features reached 92.12% frame accuracy. SF-TMN with original features reached 88.36% mean segmental F1 and an 86.69% Edit score. The difference shows why the evaluation has to match the intended use of a temporal model.

Research scope. These are offline benchmark results at one random seed. Several models use future context; the project does not establish real-time operation or clinical deployment.
